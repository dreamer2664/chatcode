## 1. The era in one view

The 176 years run from a failed Greek revolt inside the Persian Empire to the death of the man who toppled that empire. Four power transitions carry the story (Hornblower 2011, chs. 1–2; Hammond 1986):

1. **Persia vs the free cities (499–479).** The Persian Empire of Darius I was the largest state the world had known; its Greek subjects in Ionia revolted, Athens helped burn Sardis (Hdt 5.99–102), and two punitive expeditions followed (Hdt 6.43–120).
2. **The Athenian empire (478–431).** Out of the victorious alliance of Greek states grew the Delian League — allies who paid tribute (*phoros*) and became, in practice, Athens' subjects (Thuc 1.95–97; Meiggs 1972). Under Pericles this paid for the Parthenon and a direct democracy for citizens — resting on slave labour, metics and the empire's silver (Plut. Per. 12–14).
3. **The Greek civil war (431–404).** Sparta and Athens fought for twenty-seven years; plague, Sicily and Persia's gold decided it. Sparta won, and within a decade the victors quarrelled with each other and with Persia (Thuc passim; Xen. Hell. 2–5; Kagan 2003).
4. **The northern kingdom (359–323).** While the cities exhausted themselves, Philip II built the Macedonian army — the sarissa pike phalanx plus a devastating companion cavalry — and ended the free city-state at Chaeronea (Diod. 16.85–86; Ellis 1976). His son Alexander took that army to the Indus in eleven years (Arr. 1–7; Bosworth 1988).

**People the script needs.** Themistocles (the wall-builder and Salamis strategist, Hdt 7.143; 8.57–96), Aristides (the honest assessor of tribute, Thuc 1.96), Cimon (pro-Spartan victor of Eurymedon, Plut. Cim. 12–13), Pericles (the empire's voice, Thuc 2.35–46), Alcibiades (Sicily's advocate, Thuc 6.15–18), Lysander (Sparta's admiral-kingmaker, Xen. Hell. 2.1–2), Epaminondas (Thebes' hour at Leuctra, Xen. Hell. 6.4), Demosthenes (the man who saw Macedon coming, *Philippics* 1–3, 341 BC), Philip II, Alexander, Darius I, Xerxes, Mardonius, Darius III.

**Institutions worth one line each.** Athens: assembly, council, *strategoi* (elected generals), ostracism (Ath. Pol. 22; exile by potsherd, used on Themistocles himself, Plut. Them. 22). Sparta: two kings, ephors, helot underclass. Persia: satrapies, the King's Road, tribute. Macedon: an army-owning hereditary kingship with a Greek-speaking court.

## 2. Timeline (the spine)

- **499** — Ionian Revolt begins after the failed Naxos expedition; Aristagoras of Miletus defects and asks Athens for help (Hdt 5.28–38).
- **498** — Athenian force burns Sardis' lower city; retreat and defeat at Ephesus (Hdt 5.97–102).
- **494** — Persian navy wins at Lade; Miletus falls and is sacked (Hdt 6.6–20, 6.32).
- **492** — Mardonius advances through Thrace and Macedon; fleet wrecked at Athos; he is recalled (Hdt 6.43–45).
- **491** — Darius demands earth and water; most cities submit; Aegina's submission worries Athens (Hdt 6.48–49).
- **490** — Datis and Artaphernes take Eretria, land at Marathon; Athenians and Plataeans win; 192 Athenian dead (Hdt 6.94–120, casualties at 6.117). Date: August or September 490 — see §5.1. Pheidippides' run is Athens→Sparta *before* the battle (Hdt 6.105–106); see §5.12.
- **483/2** — Rich strike at Laurium; Themistocles persuades Athens to build a fleet with the silver (Hdt 7.144; Ath. Pol. 22.7 — but see §5.6).
- **481–480** — Xerxes bridges the Hellespont and cuts the Athos canal; the "wood walls" oracle divides Athens until Themistocles' fleet reading wins (Hdt 7.22–56, 7.140–144).
- **August 480** — Thermopylae: Leonidas' three hundred die with Thespians and Thebans; the epitaph ("Stranger, tell the Lacedaemonians…") (Hdt 7.201–233, epitaph 7.228). Artemisium fought in the same days (Hdt 8.1–23).
- **September 480** — Salamis: the allied fleet wins in the straits after Themistocles' secret message lures/pushes the Persians in (Hdt 8.40–96; message at 8.75 — see §5.3). Xerxes returns to Asia; Mardonius winters in Thessaly (Hdt 8.103–144).
- **August 479** — Plataea: the largest Greek land army of the war destroys Mardonius' force (Hdt 9.1–89). Mycale, a Greek naval victory on the same day *by report* (Hdt 9.90–107 — see §5.4). Winter 479/8: siege and capture of Sestos closes the invasion (Hdt 9.114–122).
- **478/7** — Delian League founded; Aristides sets the first tribute assessments (Thuc 1.94–97).
- **~467** — Cimon's double victory at the Eurymedon (Plut. Cim. 12–13; exact year uncertain).
- **465–463** — Thasos revokes tribute and is reduced; Athens fails in a supporting attack on Persia-held territory (Thuc 1.100–101).
- **454** — The League's Egyptian expedition is annihilated; the treasury moves from Delos to Athens (Thuc 1.104–110; Diod. 12.54.3; tribute lists IG I³ 259 ff.).
- **451/0** — Pericles' citizenship law: both parents must be Athenian (Ath. Pol. 26.4).
- **449?** — "Peace of Callias" with Persia: attested late and debated — see §5.5. Building programme on the Acropolis begins in this decade (Plut. Per. 12–14).
- **431** — War begins after Thebes attacks Plataea; Thucydides names Sparta's fear of Athens as the "truest cause" (Thuc 1.23.6, 2.1–2). Pericles' Funeral Oration, our classic statement of democratic Athens (Thuc 2.35–46).
- **430–426** — Plague at Athens; Thucydides survives and describes it (Thuc 2.47–54 — see §5.7). Pericles is fined, re-elected, and dies of the plague in 429 (Thuc 2.65; Diod. 12.45.4).
- **421** — Peace of Nicias: a truce neither side believes in (Thuc 5.13–24).
- **415–413** — The Sicilian expedition ends in total destruction of the Athenian force (Thuc 6.8–7.87).
- **411** — Oligarchic coup of the Four Hundred in Athens, quickly reversed (Thuc 8.63–98).
- **406** — Athens wins at Arginusae and executes its own victorious generals for failing to rescue the drowning (Xen. Hell. 1.6–7).
- **405–404** — Lysander destroys the Athenian fleet at Aegospotami; Athens capitulates; Long Walls torn down; the Thirty Tyrants installed (Xen. Hell. 2.1–2.4).
- **395–387** — Corinthian War; the "King's Peace" hands the Greek cities to Persia's guarantee — Sparta enforces it (Xen. Hell. 3–5.1).
- **371** — Epaminondas shatters Spartan power at Leuctra (Xen. Hell. 6.4.4–15).
- **362** — Mantinea: Thebes' high-water mark; Xenophon's closing lament (Xen. Hell. 7.5).
- **359** — Philip II becomes king of Macedon and rebuilds the army (Diod. 16.2–3).
- **352–346** — Philip expands into Thrace and Chalcidice; the Third Sacred War draws him south; Athens' Demosthenes organises resistance (Diod. 16.34–70; Dem. *Philippics*).
- **338** — Chaeronea: Philip's phalanx and cavalry defeat Athens and Thebes; the era of the free city-state ends in an afternoon (Diod. 16.85–86).
- **337** — League of Corinth: a "common peace" under Macedonian presidency, and a mandate to invade Persia (Diod. 16.89).
- **336** — Philip assassinated at Aegae; Alexander, twenty, is acclaimed king (Diod. 16.91–94; Arr. 1.1; see §5.8).
- **334** — Alexander crosses to Asia; first victory at the Granicus (Arr. 1.11–16).
- **333** — Issus: Darius III flees the field, leaving his family (Arr. 2.6–13).
- **332** — Siege of Tyre; Gaza falls; Egypt surrenders (Arr. 2.18–27, 3.1).
- **331** — Gaugamela breaks the Persian field army (Arr. 3.7–15); Babylon and Susa open their gates (Arr. 3.16).
- **330** — Persepolis is burned — motive contested (§5.9); Darius III is killed by his own followers (Arr. 3.18, 3.21).
- **329–327** — Two years of mountain war in Bactria and Sogdiana; Alexander kills Cleitus, who saved his life at the Granicus, in a drunken quarrel (Arr. 3.25–30, 4.7–9).
- **326** — Victory over Porus at the Hydaspes; the army mutinies at the Hyphasis river and turns back (Arr. 5.8–28).
- **324** — Mass wedding at Susa; Hephaestion dies at Ecbatana; Alexander mourns extravagantly (Arr. 7.4–6, 7.14).
- **10–11 June 323** — Alexander dies at Nebuchadnezzar's palace in Babylon, a month short of 33; cause unresolved (Arr. 7.25–26; §5.10).

## 3. Act notes for the G2 script (what is solid, what needs care)

**Act I — Ionia and Marathon.** Solid: the revolt's spark (Naxos, Aristagoras), Sardis, the collapse at Lade, Darius' demands, Marathon and its 192 (Hdt 6.117). Care: numbers — Herodotus' Persian totals are not credible and we say so; the "marathon run" as commonly told is a later construction (§5.12); the date is August or September (§5.1).
**Act II — Thermopylae and Salamis.** Solid: the Hellespont bridges, the pass and the epitaph, the strait-fight at Salamis, Xerxes' departure (Hdt 7–8). Care: Themistocles' secret message (§5.3); Persian fleet/army sizes (§5.2); the oracle's "wood walls" reading is Thucydidean-style hindsight turned into drama by Herodotus — present it as the story the Athenians told.
**Act III — Plataea.** Solid: Mardonius' winter, the battle, the end of the invasion, Sestos (Hdt 9). Care: the same-day synchronism with Mycale (§5.4).
**Act IV — Delian League and Pericles.** Solid: the founding and tribute (Thuc 1.96), Eurymedon, Egypt 454, the Acropolis programme, the Funeral Oration as counterpoint to the plague that follows it (Thuc 2.35–54). Care: Peace of Callias (§5.5); show the empire side — tribute lists, cleruchies, the citizenship law's exclusiveness.
**Act V — The Peloponnesian War.** Solid: the cause (Thuc 1.23.6), plague, Sicily, Aegospotami, 404 (Thuc; Xen. Hell.). Care: plague identity (§5.7); Pericles' death year 429 (Thuc 2.65; Diod. 12.45.4).
**Act VI — Philip and Alexander.** Solid: Chaeronea, League of Corinth, the assassination, the eleven-year campaign (Diod. 16; Arr.). Care: the motive for Philip's murder (§5.8); Persepolis (§5.9); Alexander's death (§5.10); keep the Cleitus and Hyphasis beats — they carry the arc's darkening honestly.

## 4. Numbers the script may use (with their origin)

- 192 Athenian dead, 6,400 Persian dead at Marathon, 7 ships taken (Hdt 6.117).
- 300 Spartans + allies at Thermopylae; two Spartans survive, one suicides (Hdt 7.224–232).
- Fleet sizes at Salamis: roughly 310–378 Greek triremes per source variants (Hdt 8.43–48 gives the roster); Persian fleet "1,207" assembled for the year 480 (Hdt 7.89) — moderns treat all such figures as inflated (§5.2; Green 1996, app.).
- Tribute of the Delian League at founding: 460 talents (Thuc 1.96); Athens' annual income in the 430s ~1,000 talents (later *pseudo-Xenophon*, Ath. Pol. 2.16? — cite as "the Old Oligarch").
- War length: 27 years (431–404).
- Alexander: king at 20, dead at not-quite-33; undefeated in the field (Arr. 1–7).

## 5. Uncertainties register (state these as uncertainties)

1. **Marathon's date.** August or September 490; September 12 is the conventional reconstruction (Böckh 1855; Krentz 2010 defends September; the Spartan calendar offset could pull it to August).
2. **Persian numbers.** Herodotus' totals (e.g. 1,700,000 land troops, Hdt 7.60) are impossible logistically; modern estimates run one to two orders of magnitude lower (Delbrück 1920; Green 1996; Lazenby 1993). Never quote Herodotus' grand totals as fact.
3. **Themistocles' secret message** to Xerxes before Salamis (Hdt 8.75) — staging device or historical fact; scholars divide (Lazenby 1993; Green 1996). Present as "Herodotus reports."
4. **Plataea/Mycale same-day** (Hdt 9.100–101) — suspiciously neat synchronism; flag it.
5. **Peace of Callias (449?).** Unmentioned by Thucydides, attested by Diodorus (12.4) and the 4th-century orators; existence still debated (Meiggs 1972, ch. 9 accepts with conditions; Hornblower 2011 is agnostic).
6. **Laurium ships: 100 or 200** — Hdt 7.144 vs Ath. Pol. 22.7. Say "a fleet," or cite both.
7. **The plague's identity.** DNA from a Kerameikos mass grave suggests typhoid (Papagrigorakis et al. 2006, *Int. J. Infect. Dis.* 10.3: 206–14), but the identification is disputed (Shapiro et al. 2006 and others); Thucydides' description fits no modern disease exactly. "Probably typhoid, still debated" is the honest line.
8. **Philip's assassination motive** — Pausanias' personal grudge (bodyguard dispute) vs wider conspiracy (Olympias? Alexander? Persia?), no settled answer (Diod. 16.91–94; Ellis 1976).
9. **Persepolis burned** — revenge for 480 with the Persians' own palace-ware (Arr. 3.18.12), or drunken impulse prompted by the Athenian courtesan Thais (Curt. 5.6.7)? The Thais tale is likely embellishment.
10. **Alexander's death** — fever (typhoid/malaria), alcoholism complications, poisoning, or — a recent clinical proposal — Guillain–Barré syndrome explaining the reported delayed decomposition (Arr. 7.25–26 for the facts; Bosworth 1988 for the traditions; Hall 2018, *Ancient History Bulletin* 32: 106–28 for GBS). No consensus.
11. **Funeral Oration vs the plague** — the juxtaposition is real (both in Book 2), but Pericles' actual words are Thucydides' composition; say so if the script quotes it.
12. **The "marathon run."** Herodotus has Pheidippides run Athens→Sparta *before* the battle (Hdt 6.105–106). The post-battle Marathon→Athens run and "nenikikamen" story appear only in Plutarch (*Theseus* 3? and *Moralia*) and Lucian — centuries later. The race name is modern (1896). Use carefully.
