# The training loop — learn by shipping and revising

This campaign trains agents in the full craft, not in writing impressive task
reports. A worker must leave enough evidence that a stranger can judge the film
and reproduce its production. Read ACCEPTANCE.md first.

## Common lessons and deliverables

**V01 — editorial judgment and research.** Build the ten-topic slate with distinct
viewer promises. Show why sources are credible, what a primary source changes,
what a tempting weak claim looks like, and how to replace it. Deliver ten timed
outlines, claim/rights templates, collision checks and a real investigated sample.
Owners can improve the proposed topics; final approval is recorded, not assumed.

**V02 — reliable long-form production.** Extend the existing 30-second renderer by
orchestrating bounded shots, not by deleting resource limits. Assemble 300 seconds
of real ordered picture, narration and captions with FFmpeg. Cache by content hash,
resume after an interrupted shot, check A/V timing and resource usage, and reject
truncated/loop-padded/broken artifacts. The engineering calibration film is labeled
as a fixture and does not count among the ten editorial productions.

**V03 — voice, listening, captions and mixing.** Compare legitimate available voice
routes, actually listen, choose one usable route, and demonstrate a full-length
narration/alignment/mix. Record pronunciation repairs, chunk seams, breaths/pauses,
caption offsets and speaker tests. Synthetic speech must sound intelligible and
natural enough to pass independent listening, not merely exist as a WAV. No paid
service, copied credentials or imitated real person is presumed available.

**V04 — visual direction and honest quality review.** Create a style system that
supports different film types, with examples of composition, diagrams, charts,
legibility, transitions and motion. Make and critique representative pilot sequences,
show revisions, and provide a calibrated full-watch review workflow. An entropy or
spring-physics score cannot certify that a film is good. Leave reusable building
blocks, not ten identical layout presets.

## Every individual production repeats this cycle

1. **Pitch/reserve.** Select an exact topic, intended viewer, promise, format and
   failure risk. Check slate collisions. Explain what this film will teach its maker.
2. **Research.** Collect at least three credible sources. Write a claim-to-source
   ledger and asset rights ledger. If a claim/asset cannot be verified, replace it.
3. **Script.** Read it aloud, time it, cut filler, check comprehension and pacing.
   Record at least two substantive editorial changes from the first draft.
4. **Storyboard.** Timecode the whole film, match every scene to narration, specify
   assets and explanatory purpose, plan captions/phone legibility before rendering.
5. **Produce.** Record/synthesize approved speech, build actual scenes and meaningful
   demonstrations, edit sound and picture. Render a complete rough cut, not a teaser.
6. **Receive critique.** A non-owner watches/listens and leaves timestamped notes.
   No vague "looks good" and no scoring your own film as independent evidence.
7. **Revise.** Resolve every critical/major note; document at least three meaningful
   changes where needed. Explain any rejected suggestion with evidence. Do not fake
   defects merely to increase a revision count.
8. **Final QC.** Run mechanical checks and two full-watch editorial reviews bound to
   the final hash. Re-render and re-review if the bytes change. A blocked voice or
   unavailable licensed asset is an honest blocker, not a placeholder permission.
9. **Deliver.** Deposit real retrievable media on storagecode Releases, archive small
   evidence and the release manifest, update the viewing index and your card.
10. **Teach the next agent.** A short postmortem: before/after examples, a mistake you
    corrected, the reusable rule, observed resource cost and next improvement. Report
    observed facts, not fabricated retention, watch-time or monetisation projections.

## Formats for the ten slots (topics remain the agents' choice)

- **YT01:** evidence-led explainer — make an abstract mechanism visibly understandable.
- **YT02:** real screen demonstration/tutorial — the steps must work in a clean setup.
- **YT03:** mini-documentary — chronology, rights and uncertainty without invented scenes.
- **YT04:** data story — source a dataset, reproduce calculations, avoid misleading charts.
- **YT05:** how-a-system-works — a consistent diagrammatic model with honest simplifications.
- **YT06:** misconception investigation — strongest fair statement, evidence, correction.
- **YT07:** practical build/walkthrough — a demonstrable outcome and a reproducible recipe.
- **YT08:** object/innovation biography — causality and context, not a list of trivia.
- **YT09:** visual thought experiment — assumptions explicit; simulation labeled as simulation.
- **YT10:** original-format capstone — apply lessons without copying another slot's treatment.

Different slots may share code, voice and design primitives. They may not share
essentially the same script, claim set or timeline. Retopic a slot rather than
stretching a weak idea to five minutes.

## Leases and handoffs

These are multi-shift jobs, not ten-minute patches. Typical effort: shared jobs
6–16 focused hours, individual films 8–20, final screening 6–12. These are estimates,
not artificial minimum timers. Claim a bounded lease (for example eight hours),
heartbeat at real checkpoints and leave a handoff with exact source/asset paths,
completed stage, rough-cut link, commands, measurements, critiques and next steps.

Do not hold the Director's computer while researching, rendering or waiting for
review. This batch works without the paused Mars bridge. No unapproved purchases,
voice cloning, scraping of private sessions or public YouTube upload shortcuts.
