# YT09 — Check Into Hilbert's Hotel

**Format:** visual thought experiment (V13 card `t-20261015-e14c`) · 300–330 s · slot YT09

## Audience and promise

A curious viewer with no mathematics past school. Promise: **you will watch a
completely full hotel with infinitely many rooms take in one more guest, then a
bus of infinitely many, then infinitely many buses — and you will understand why
none of that is a trick.** The film's discipline: every move the hotel makes is
shown, counted and never hand-waved.

## Sources (three, one primary)

1. **Primary:** David Hilbert, "Über das Unendliche" (lecture delivered
   1925–1926; published *Mathematische Annalen* 95, 1926) — the hotel appears in
   this treatment of the actual infinite. The film quotes only what it can show.
2. George Gamow, *One Two Three… Infinity* (1947) — the book that made the hotel
   famous; credited on screen as the popularisation, not the origin.
3. A standard open course text on countability and Cantor's diagonal argument
   (for example an openly licensed discrete-mathematics or set-theory chapter),
   used for the formal statement of the moves and cited for the diagonal step.

## Claim ledger (material claims only)

| # | claim in film | source | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A countably infinite set can be put in one-to-one correspondence with a proper subset of itself | Hilbert 1926; any standard text | 1:30 |
| 2 | The shift n→n+1 frees exactly one room; n→2n frees all odd-numbered rooms | arithmetic shown on screen | 1:30, 2:15 |
| 3 | Countably many countable sets are still countable (the bus-of-buses move) | standard result, cited text | 3:00 |
| 4 | The real numbers are not countable, so some arrivals cannot be housed | Cantor's diagonal argument, cited | 3:50 |

Uncertainty is marked: the film attributes the *hotel story* to Hilbert's
lecture tradition as reported by Gamow, and does not claim to quote a line from
the 1926 paper that it has not verified in the source it can actually open.
