# YT06 — The 10% Brain Myth: Anatomy of a Bad Idea

**Format:** misconception investigation (V10 card `t-20261015-67df`) · 300–330 s · slot YT06

This pitch doubles as the campaign's **detailed research example**: it shows the
standard the other nine dossiers are held to — checked passages, a named
rejection, and a claim ledger that maps every material statement to a source.
See `../research-example-YT06.md`.

## Audience and promise

A viewer who has heard "we only use 10% of our brains" in a classroom, a
motivational video or a film trailer. Promise: **you will be able to trace this
one false claim from a vague sentence in 1907 to a bestselling foreword in 1936
to the movies — and you will see exactly where each retelling added something
that was never there.**

The film's craft is the *lineage*, not the debunking. Anyone can say it's false;
showing the four hops where the number was invented is the story.

## Sources (three, one primary)

1. **Primary:** William James, "The Energies of Men" (1907/1908) — the sentence
   the myth is built on, in the public domain and freely readable.
   https://www.gutenberg.org (James essays) — the film quotes it verbatim.
2. Barry L. Beyerstein, "Do We Really Use Only 10 Percent of the Brain?" — the
   standard scholarly account of the lineage, including the Einstein-archives
   search that found no such statement.
   https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/do-we-really-use-only-10/
3. Beyerstein, "Whence Cometh the Myth That We Only Use 10% of Our Brains?" in
   *Skeptical Inquirer* / *The Left Brain–Right Brain Story* (1999) — the fuller
   treatment, cited for the "silent cortex" hop.

## Claim ledger (material claims only)

| # | claim in film | source | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | James wrote about unused *potential*, never a percentage of brain | James 1907, quoted on screen | 0:45 |
| 2 | Thomas's 1936 Carnegie foreword attributed "10 percent of latent mental ability" to James | Beyerstein 2004, citing the foreword | 1:30 |
| 3 | Early "silent cortex" language was misread as unused brain | Beyerstein 1999 | 2:15 |
| 4 | The Einstein attribution is unsupported — archive staff found no evidence | Beyerstein 2004 | 3:00 |
| 5 | Brain imaging shows widespread activity; the brain is ~2% of body mass using a large share of its energy | Beyerstein 2004, imaging literature | 3:45 |

Uncertainty is labelled: the film says plainly that the *relative* share of
energy figures vary between sources and cites the range it uses rather than a
single rounded number.

## Rights / asset strategy

No film clips, no celebrity images, no music beyond a licensed/CC0 bed. The
1936 foreword is quoted in short excerpt for commentary, with the book credited
on screen. Brain diagrams are original drawings, not lifted anatomical plates.

## Collision note

## Known blockers

None. The single risk is over-claiming the neuroimaging side; the script keeps
that segment to findings the cited review states directly and marks everything
else as "often repeated, not established here."
