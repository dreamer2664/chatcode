# YT08 — Ten Lines That Swallowed Retail: The Barcode

**Format:** object / innovation biography (V12 card `t-20261015-eeca`) · 300–330 s · slot YT08

## Audience and promise

Anyone who has stood at a checkout and never looked twice at the symbol on the
pack. Promise: **the barcode was invented twice over — once as a bull's-eye
patent in 1952 that nobody bought, and again as the UPC that beeped in an Ohio
supermarket in 1974 — and the inventors sold the first one for fifteen thousand
dollars.** A biography with a real emotional shape: early idea, long neglect,
sudden ubiquity, no fortune.

## Sources (three, one primary)

1. **Primary:** U.S. Patent 2,612,994, *Classifying Apparatus and Method*
   (N. J. Woodland and B. Silver; filed October 20, 1949; granted October 7,
   1952) — the original drawings are public domain and are the film's visual
   centrepiece. https://patents.google.com/patent/US2612994
2. Smithsonian National Museum of American History — the actual first-scanned
   pack of Wrigley's gum is in the collection, which is the strongest available
   evidence for the 1974 event. https://americanhistory.si.edu
3. Oral-history and press accounts of the June 26, 1974 scan at the Marsh
   Supermarket in Troy, Ohio (cashier Sharon Buchanan; Clyde Dawson of Marsh) —
   used for the human detail, each attributed on screen.
