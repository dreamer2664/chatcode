# YT02 — Verify Anything: SHA-256 in the Real World

**Format:** real screen tutorial (V06 card `t-20261015-4b76`) · 300–330 s · slot YT02

## Audience and promise

A viewer who downloads files, follows install instructions or keeps backups, and
has typed `sha256sum` once without knowing what it proved. Promise: **by the end
you can check any file's integrity yourself and say precisely what that check
does and does not prove.** That last clause is the film's spine — a hash proves
sameness, never safety or authorship.

## Why this over the alternatives

It is genuinely filmable with assets we control: one terminal, one real machine,
files we generate on camera. No stock footage, no borrowed screencasts, no
claimed results. The failure modes are reproducible on demand.

## Sources (three, one primary)

1. **Primary:** NIST FIPS PUB 180-4, *Secure Hash Standard (SHS)*, March 2012 —
   defines SHA-256 and fixes the 256-bit (64 hex char) digest.
   https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.180-4.pdf
2. NIST CSRC glossary entry for *checksum* / *cryptographic hash* — the
   distinction the film leans on. https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary
3. Git's own documentation on object naming (`git help hash-object` / the
   Pro Git "Git Objects" chapter, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0) — a real checksum-in-anger
   example, credited per its licence. https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2/Git-Internals-Git-Objects

## Claim ledger (material claims only)

| # | claim in film | source | where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SHA-256 outputs 256 bits, written as 64 hex characters | FIPS 180-4 | 0:45 |
| 2 | a one-bit change alters the digest unpredictably (avalanche) | FIPS 180-4 §properties + on-camera demo | 1:30 |
| 3 | a matching digest does not prove who made the file | reasoning from (1)+(2), stated as reasoning | 3:00 |
| 4 | git names its objects by hash | Pro Git, credited on screen | 3:45 |

Uncertainty is labelled: the film says explicitly that collision resistance is a
property of the algorithm, not of any single comparison you perform.
